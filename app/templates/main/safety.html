Never pay to apply or interview.

Stop if someone requests an application fee, training deposit, equipment payment, gift card, cryptocurrency, bank PIN, card CVV, or one-time password.

Before you continue

A five-minute verification checklist

Use these checks even when a listing looks polished or urgent.

01
{{ icon('search', 23) }}

Find the employer independently

Type the organisation's website address yourself and look for the same role on its official careers page.

02
{{ icon('mail', 23) }}

Inspect the contact

Check the complete sender address. A free email domain or misspelled company domain deserves extra verification.

03
{{ icon('file', 23) }}

Read the job carefully

Compare the title, duties, location, salary, experience, and application deadline across sources.

04
{{ icon('lock', 23) }}

Limit personal data

Do not share identity documents, bank credentials, tax IDs, or account codes before independently verifying the need and recipient.

05
{{ icon('user', 23) }}

Slow down pressure

Legitimate employers allow questions. Do not let an urgent offer, secrecy request, or text-only interview rush you.

06
{{ icon('shield', 23) }}

Report concerns

Use Report listing on the job detail page so the role can enter the administrator review queue.

{{ icon('alert', 21) }}

Common warning signs

  • ×Money required before an application, interview, or offer.
  • ×A cheque followed by instructions to buy equipment or return funds.
  • ×Requests for an OTP, PIN, password, card CVV, or remote device access.
  • ×An offer without a credible interview or written role details.
  • ×Recruiters who refuse verifiable company contact information.
  • ×Links with misspelled domains or unexpected downloadable software.
If something feels wrong

Stop the conversation and preserve evidence.

  1. 1
    Do not send more information or money.

    Leave the page or chat and do not open further files.

  2. 2
    Save useful evidence.

    Keep the listing URL, sender address, messages, and payment request.

  3. 3
    Contact the organisation independently.

    Use contact details from its verified website, not from the suspicious message.

  4. 4
    Protect exposed accounts.

    Change affected passwords and contact your bank or relevant authority if sensitive data or money was shared.
